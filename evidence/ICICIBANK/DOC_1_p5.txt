Thank you very much, sir. We will now begin the Q&A session with Mr. Batra and Mr.
Banerjee. Anyone who wishes to ask a question may press ‘*’ and ‘1’ on their telephone.
If you wish to remove yourself from the question queue you may press ‘*’ and ‘2’. Today's
announcement is on the Bank's financial performance. Hence, we would like to request
you to ask questions related to that. Please contact the Corporate Communications team
separately for any other queries.
Due to time constraints, I request all of you to ask two questions at a time. If you have
additional queries, you may join the queue again, if time permits. Thank you.
We will take our first question from Ritu Singh from CNBC-TV18. Please go ahead.
Ritu Singh:
Thanks very much for that, and good evening, Mr. Batra. I just quickly wanted to start by
understanding what has happened on the margin front. That 18-basis point decline.
Could you give us an explanation, how much is because of the FCNR flows and how much
is because of this one-time tax refund, in the first quarter? Where do you see margins
sort of stabilising? That is the first question.
Also, the sustainability of your deposit and credit growth. Almost 27-28% year-on-year
deposit growth, but excluding FCNR, deposits just grew by 17%. Again, the loan book
from 30% would have fallen to about 24% excluding the loans by international branches
against these FCNR deposits. So where are you seeing growth? How do you see that
stabilising as well? And a quick point, if you could add on your slippages of ₹4,833 crore,
where they came from? Anything you would like to flag?
Sandeep Batra:
That is three questions, Ritu, but let me answer them. As we have reported, the net
interest income increased by about 17.7% year-on-year and about 3.9% sequentially. The
NIM was 4.18% for this quarter compared to 4.36% in the previous quarter. As I had
mentioned, if I exclude the interest on tax refund, it was 4.28% in the previous quarter.
So, we are really talking of a decline from 4.28% to 4.18%. That is largely a function of
FCNR(B) which accordingly results in a higher deposit amount and a lower CASA. As you
would have seen, our CASA ratio has marginally declined, which has been responsible
for the marginal decline in the NIMs. The large sequential increase in retail term deposits
and increase in overseas branches of total assets has also led to moderation in margins.
The full impact would largely get reflected in the third quarter, after which we expect the
margins to start moving up, even based on the current level of policy rates.
